The experience

Two short steps.
A human follow-up.

The same quick form is available across English and Spanish landing pages. The full Get Offer questionnaire remains available for visitors who prefer to provide all the details.

1

Identify the vehicle

Choose Year / Make / Model from dropdowns, or enter a VIN. Once a valid 17-character VIN is entered, the form looks up its vehicle details.

17-character VIN → NHTSA lookup → editable year, make & model

A warning remains visible when the provider cannot fully verify a VIN. If lookup fails, visitors can enter the vehicle details themselves. Changing the VIN clears the previous vehicle data.

Makes cover the car and truck catalog. Models depend on the selected year and make. Older or unlisted vehicles can use manual entry.

2

Add the basics & send

Two screening questions help the team prepare for the call, followed by the seller’s name, phone number, and pickup ZIP.

Does the car run?
Yes · No · Not sure
Ownership & title
Owner with title · Owner without title · Authorized to sell · Not sure

Every answer remains eligible for human review. These are seller-reported details; they do not establish ownership, condition, eligibility, or a cash offer.

English & Spanish, one consistent flow

The shared quick form appears in the built HTML for 120 content pages, plus the not-found view. It offers clear field errors, recovery from lookup failures, and a visible choice between VIN lookup and dropdowns.

The detailed Get Offer form is preserved

The longer questionnaire retains its pickup, contact, title, and condition questions. Its retry handling now includes a stable submission UUID, a 30-second timeout, and an explicit success-response check.

Before production

Protect the lead.
Measure the outcome.

The local implementation is ready to review. These operational checks matter before sending paid traffic to a production release.

Current boundary: no deployment or actual inbox-delivery verification has been completed. Passing local tests does not establish production delivery or a conversion increase.

  1. Confirm security & real receipt

    Verify the production Turnstile site/secret pair and allowed domains, then confirm a controlled inquiry and detailed offer reach the intended inbox. Check the sender, recipient, and reply-to behavior.

  2. Give every lead a durable record

    Store requests in a CRM or database before relying on email. Add delivery retries, bounce/failure handling, and a clear follow-up owner. Provider acceptance alone does not confirm inbox receipt.

  3. Make data handling clear

    Add an accessible privacy link near submission. Explain the contact purpose, who can access the data, and how long it is retained. Keep seller details out of general analytics events.

  4. Connect events to qualified leads

    Verify tags for quick_inquiry_submit_success and offer_form_submit_success. Track the two paths separately and connect them to qualification and purchases. Judge improvement by cost per qualified lead, with completed forms as a supporting measure.

  5. Bound public submissions

    Add request-size limits and submission-rate limits. Keep server validation, Turnstile checks, and clear retry messages in place so abuse controls do not block ordinary sellers.

  6. Finish real-device checks

    Test iPhone Safari and Android Chrome: keyboard entry, dropdown changes, VIN correction, security completion, weak connections, and retry. Include small screens and the English and Spanish flows.
